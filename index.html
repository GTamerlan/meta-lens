EXPLODED OPTICAL STUDY NOT TO SCALE

LAYER 01 / 05

Comfort is the starting point.

A contact-lens surface designed around fit, oxygen transmission and a stable optical position. The goal is less bulk on the face—not an assumption that every eye will find it comfortable.

THE PROOF REQUIRED

Demonstrate safe wear, reliable fit and sustained comfort with qualified clinical partners.

Illustrative architecture, not a manufacturing blueprint. Layer sizes, materials, arrangement and visual effects are conceptual. “Nano” is not a shortcut around power, optics or eye safety.

A tiny interface. A connected system.

The lens doesn’t work alone.

Keep heavy computation, connectivity and much of the sensing off the eye. A companion device would help run the experience; safe power delivery, input and reliable spatial tracking still need to be proven together.

01 / HUMAN FIRST

Prove the wear.

Eye safety. Oxygen. Fit. Comfort over time. Begin with eligible adult contact-lens wearers and qualified clinical oversight.

Behind the concept

Ambition. With context.

This is a student concept visualization accompanying a YUCG presentation. It is not a working smart contact lens, a medical recommendation or a Meta announcement.

What you are seeing

The 3D lens is an illustrative five-part architecture, not a validated device. The lifestyle scenes and interfaces are staged concept visuals. World-locked content would require reliable head/world tracking as well as eye tracking; opaque-looking panels here are a storytelling device, not demonstrated contact-lens optics.

The important boundaries

Wearability, oxygen delivery, heat, power, contrast, hygiene, input, manufacturability and regulatory authorization remain essential research questions. A contact lens is not automatically more comfortable—or more desirable—than glasses. Sharing a workspace would need an external camera and permission. Spatial product previews need correct 3D assets and tracking.
