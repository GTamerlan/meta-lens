Deep dive: what the custom electronics would do

Control ASIC: a custom chip could integrate clocking, link handling, a small frame buffer, display timing, power-state control and sensor readout. Integration saves the board area and interconnect overhead of separate catalog packages, but introduces design cost, verification, silicon yield and test-access problems.

Display path: receive a packet → validate it → update a buffer or compact scene description → drive pixels with controlled timing and brightness. A lost packet must not create an uncontrolled bright output. A watchdog, conservative brightness cap, brownout handling and default-off state are proposed requirements—not implemented hardware.

Interfaces: SPI and I²C can connect conventional bench modules. They do not define the final on-lens interconnect, voltage domains, packaging or radio. Specify those after measuring real loads, tolerances and signal integrity.

Software split: keep social accounts, large models and scene rendering off the eye. Use authenticated commands, bounded queues and expiry times. A stale instruction should disappear instead of freezing indefinitely in the user’s view. Our UI is a demonstration of this concept, not firmware.

Efficiency here covers the entire assumed transfer path, including transmitter, coupling, receiver and regulation. We do not assign a frequency, coil geometry, field strength or exposure limit. A real design must measure alignment sensitivity, blink/motion effects, conversion losses, transient peaks, standby, battery ageing and protective shutdown. Optical output also carries energy: electrical power is not synonymous with local tissue heating.

On-lens storage follows the same energy arithmetic: nominal µWh ≈ nominal volts × µAh. Actual usable energy depends on the discharge curve and load. A calculated runtime does not establish that a battery fits, delivers the necessary current, or can safely be sealed into a lens.

Which architecture should be tested?

Compare off-eye power with a passive/harvested endpoint, a small on-lens buffer, and a sealed microbattery alternative. No option automatically wins. The choice depends on measured peak current, link reliability, packaging, eye safety and usable energy—not nominal capacity alone.

Deep dive: bandwidth, latency, angular resolution and safety states

Don’t confuse a radio rate with usable payload. A catalog nRF52840 supports a 2 Mbps Bluetooth LE mode. That is not evidence it can stream these raw frames; protocol overhead reduces usable throughput. Local rendering of simple commands, compression, sparse updates and a different custom link are architecture options. Each changes computation, latency and verification requirements. Nordic ↗

Pixels per degree (PPD): horizontal pixels divided by horizontal field of view is a rough angular-sampling estimate. For a hypothetical 640-pixel image spanning 20°, this is 32 pixels/degree. That calculation says nothing by itself about optical MTF, retinal image quality, pupil coverage or safe brightness.

Latency chain: sensing → pose estimation → rendering → encoding → transfer → buffering → scanout → light. Average latency alone hides jitter and stale frames. Measure the full distribution during motion, link dropouts and thermal throttling. Do not substitute a network ping for motion-to-photon performance.

Safety states: proposed firmware should treat over-brightness, invalid timing, power instability, excessive temperature and lost supervision as reasons to disable the display. An independently reviewed hardware shutdown path is different from a software “off” button. Thresholds must come from device-specific hazard analysis and validation—not this website.

Small displays give us a starting point.

Microdisplays concentrate pixels into a small active area. The integration task is to combine that light source with optics, drivers, interconnects and protective barriers in a lens-compatible geometry.

Carrier, water content and oxygen transport

A lens carrier must satisfy optical and biological requirements together. Candidate soft materials include hydrogel or silicone-hydrogel systems; rigid gas-permeable approaches have different fitting and mechanical behavior. Selection belongs with qualified lens designers, not a cosmetic preference.

Dk versus Dk/t: oxygen permeability is a material property; transmissibility also depends on thickness. An electronic island can change the local transport path even when the surrounding lens material performs well. Evaluate the complete device, including barriers, adhesives, surface treatments and geometry. This page deliberately gives no universal “safe” thickness or wear duration.

Encapsulation is a system, not a coating trick

Electronics need protection from moisture and ions. The eye needs protection from conductors, electrochemistry and leachable materials. Thin barrier layers, polymer encapsulation and compliant interconnects are research tools—not interchangeable eye-safe ingredients.

Evaluate barrier defects, edge seals, pinholes, swelling, cracks after flexing, corrosion, delamination and changes after cleaning or sterilization. Confirm optical transmission and oxygen transport after ageing, not just on a pristine coupon. A materials data sheet is not biocompatibility evidence for the assembled device.

Nanophotonics, interconnects and fabrication yield

A metasurface shapes light with subwavelength structure. In a 2024 AR-glasses study, researchers reported a 384 nm grating period and 220 nm height. The demonstrated system still used a millimeter-scale waveguide and external components. It is a concrete example of nanophotonic function, not an electronic contact lens. Primary paper ↗

Fine lithographic features require process control and inspection. In a proposed lens program, test coupons should precede curved integration; alignment, transfer, bonding and sealing each introduce yield losses. A beautiful single prototype does not establish reproducible manufacture.

Possible routes such as lithography, thin-film deposition, transfer assembly and nanoimprinting require appropriate facilities and process development. This page describes the engineering sequence, not chemical recipes or a fabrication process qualified for eye contact.
